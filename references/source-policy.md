# Source policy

Research before teaching domain claims. Use the learner's conversation as
context, not as an authority.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order appropriate to the domain:

1. official curricula, standards, specifications, or documentation;
2. university course materials and open textbooks;
3. established textbooks or recognized reference works;
4. public exams, problem sets, test suites, and authentic practice;
5. reputable practitioner material for examples and operational wisdom.

A syllabus defines scope, not a complete pedagogy. Compare sources when the
ordering, prerequisites, or claimed workload materially affect the roadmap.

## Record

For every resource, record title, author or owner, URL or bibliographic detail,
access date, purpose, trust reason, and whether it is curriculum, explanation,
practice, assessment, or community wisdom. Mark unverified candidates.

## Copyright and attribution

Synthesize explanations in original language. Cite sources near supported
claims. Use short excerpts only when necessary. Do not reproduce textbooks,
answer banks, or proprietary course materials in lesson or module PDFs. Prefer
openly licensed material for reusable exercises and diagrams.

If reliable sources are unavailable or disagree, state the uncertainty and
narrow the claim or roadmap rather than manufacturing authority.
